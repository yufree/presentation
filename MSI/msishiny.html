Exploring Kidney Metabolism: An Interactive Network Tool

The Challenge: Making Sense of Complex Metabolic Data

  • Kidneys perform countless chemical reactions involving many small molecules (metabolites).
  • Modern techniques like Mass Spectrometry generate huge amounts of data about these metabolites.
  • Mass Spectrometry Imaging (MSI) even shows us where these metabolites are located in the tissue.
  • Challenge: How do we see the relationships between these metabolites and understand potential reaction pathways within this complex data?

Our Solution: An Interactive Metabolic Network Tool

  • We’ve built a web-based tool to help visualize and explore these relationships.
  • Think of it as an interactive map of potential metabolic connections in your kidney data.
  • Goal: To help you generate hypotheses, explore connections, and understand your data better, without needing to code!

What You’re Looking At: The Basics

  • Dots (Nodes): Represent individual Metabolites.
    • Labeled with their chemical name if identified.
    • Labeled with m/z_ccs (mass/charge and collision cross-section) if unidentified.
  • Lines (Edges): Represent a potential Relationship or Reaction between two metabolites.

How Are Metabolites Linked?

  1. Paired Mass Differences (PMDs):
    • Many biochemical reactions involve adding or removing small, common chemical groups (like water, methyl, phosphate).
    • The difference in mass between two connected metabolites often matches the mass of these common groups.
    • We call this difference a PMD. It suggests a potential biochemical transformation.

How Are Metabolites Linked?

  1. Spatial Correlation (from MSI):
    • Metabolites involved in the same pathway or located in the same cell type often appear in similar locations in the kidney tissue image.
    • The lines also reflect this spatial co-localization – stronger correlation means they are found together more often.

Let’s Tour the Tool: Main Sections

  1. Information Panel: Instructions and contact info.
  2. Data Exploration Tabs: Where you find reactions and search.
    • Reactions (PMDs) Table
    • Sample PMD Search
    • KEGG PMD Search
  3. Network Visualization: The main interactive map area.
  4. Metabolite Information: Shows details (like MSI images) for selected metabolites.

Step 1: Explore Potential Reactions (PMD Table)

  • Go to the Data Exploration -> Reactions (PMDs) Table tab.
  • This table shows common Mass Differences (PMDs) found frequently in your kidney dataset.

Step 1: Explore Potential Reactions (PMD Table)

  • Key Columns:
    • pmd: The mass difference value.
    • freq: How many metabolite pairs in your data have this mass difference.
    • reaction: The likely biochemical reaction type.
    • freq_kegg: How common this PMD is in known biological reactions (KEGG database).
  • ACTION: Select rows corresponding to reactions you want to investigate further. Click on the rows.

Step 2: View the Network

  • Once you’ve selected one or more rows (reactions) in the table…

  • ACTION: Click the big blue “Show Network” button above the network panel.

  • The network map will appear, showing ONLY the metabolites and relationships corresponding to the reactions you selected.

Step 3: Explore the Interactive Network

  • Interact: Zoom in/out, pan around, drag metabolites (dots) to rearrange the view.
  • Hover over a Line (Edge): A tooltip appears showing the exact PMD value and the spatial correlation score between the two connected metabolites.
  • ACTION: Click on a Dot (Metabolite) that interests you.

Step 4: See Where Metabolites Are Located (MSI View)

  • When you click on a metabolite (dot) in the network…
  • Look at the right-hand panel.
  • An MSI Image appears, showing the spatial distribution of that specific metabolite across the kidney tissue slice.
  • A color bar (White -> Red) indicates low to high abundance.

Step 5: Find a Specific Metabolite

  • Want to find a known metabolite (e.g., Citrate) or an unidentified feature (m/z_ccs) in the current network view?
  • ACTION: Use the “Select or Search Node (Metabolite)” dropdown menu above the MSI image.
  • Start typing the name or m/z_ccs.
  • Select it from the list. The tool will highlight it in the network and show its MSI image.
  • (Note: It only finds metabolites present in the network you generated in Step 2)

Bonus Feature: Deeper Dive with PMD Search

  • Want to investigate a specific mass difference (PMD) that wasn’t in the main table, or explore it more?
  • Use the Data Exploration tabs:
    1. Sample PMD Search:
      • Enter a PMD value (e.g., 14.016 for Methylation) and a tolerance (e.g., 0.001).
      • Click Search.
      • Result: Shows all metabolite pairs in your kidney data with that mass difference.

Bonus Feature: Deeper Dive with PMD Search

  • Use the Data Exploration tabs:

    1. KEGG PMD Search:
      • Enter a PMD value and tolerance.
      • Click Search.
      • Result: Shows known biochemical reactions from the KEGG database that match this mass difference. Useful for identifying potential reaction types.

  • ACTION: You can Download these search results as CSV files.

Why is This Tool Useful for Biologists?

  • Visualize Connections: See potential metabolic pathways and relationships directly from your data.
  • Generate Hypotheses: Identify candidate reactions occurring in your kidney samples (e.g., “Is hydroxylation active here?”).
  • Explore Neighbors: Find out what other metabolites are related (by PMD or location) to a metabolite you’re studying.
  • Link Unknowns: Connect unidentified features (m/z_ccs) to known metabolites through shared PMDs.
  • Spatial Context: Instantly see where metabolites in the network are located in the tissue via MSI.
